The engine

i
One-time step. The engine is the wllama build of llama.cpp compiled to WebAssembly — two files, wllama.js (the single-file ES module build) and wllama.wasm (single-threaded). Download them once, exactly as you download models, drop them in here, and they are stored inside the app. Bake writes a copy of this app with the engine inside it — one file that needs nothing else, ever. The app itself never downloads anything.
!
Set expectations. A standalone page cannot run multithreaded WebAssembly (that requires server headers, which by definition we do not have). Single-threaded is genuinely capable: small models (1B–3B, Q4) reply at roughly reading speed on a decent machine; 7B will crawl, and a low powered tablet may not manage even small models well. The fit warnings below are your guide.

On file types. GGUF is what the engine runs directly (llama architecture models — Llama, Mistral and similar families share it). PyTorch files (.pt, .pth, .bin) and .safetensors need a one time conversion to GGUF with separate tools. The Foundry reads each GGUF header and tells you its architecture.

What The Foundry is

A single, self contained page that runs on this device with no connection. It is a home for local AI models: bring them in, name them, set how they run, and talk to them. Your work stays here.

1
Add the engine once. On the Models page, drop in the two engine files (wllama.js and wllama.wasm), downloaded exactly as you download models. Bake them into the app file if you want one file to carry.
2
Add a model. Drop in a GGUF you downloaded. The Foundry reads its header, checks it against this device, and keeps a link to it.
3
Load and chat. Set the model active, go to Chat, press Load. Replies stream in as they are produced.

Honest limits

!
Speed. Single-threaded WebAssembly only — a standalone page cannot do better. Small models feel fine; big ones crawl. A low powered tablet may struggle with even small models.
i
Formats. The engine runs GGUF files with llama-family architectures. Everything else needs converting to GGUF first, outside the app.
?
Memory. Browsers under-report memory. The fit check is cautious; if something borderline fails to load, the app will say so plainly.

The instruments

S
Schedae is Latin for leaves of paper. The notepad button, the panel, and every note in one place: pin one to the page, search the rest, and send any of them into Tabula when a thought is ready to become writing.
C
Clepsydra, the water clock, counts your focus in short sprints with short and long breaks between, and tallies the day. Settings set the lengths, the chime, and whether the hourglass shows the live countdown.
T
Tabula is the writing tablet, on the slate stone, and Abacus the reckoning grid on the green one, with small formulas and CSV in and out. Both float over the page, move, resize from their edges, and fold into the crystal key at the floor of the page.
✦
The crystal key, low on the left, appears when an instrument is afloat. Its stones show what is open; a line brings that instrument forward or lets it go.
